# Publishing a Story-Forge series on YouTube

Policy notes for a stick-figure animated history channel with synthetic
narration and AI-generated stills. Checked against YouTube's Help Center on
2026-10-20; the platform changes these pages, so re-read the two sources before
a launch decision.

## 1. AI disclosure

YouTube requires disclosure only for content that **seems realistic**:

- a real person appears to say or do something they did not do;
- footage of a real event or place is altered;
- a **realistic** scene is generated that did not happen;
- AI-generated **music** is the main focus.

Non-realistic animation is explicitly exempt. The Help Center's own example is
"using an AI-generated or altered animation … in a fully animated video", and
minor assistance — scripts, outlines, thumbnails, captions, upscaling, voice
repair — needs no disclosure either.

**For this channel:** a stick-figure history episode is non-realistic
animation, so the AI-use setting can be "No". Two caveats:

- The synthetic narration is not a cloned real person's voice; if a future
  episode imitates a specific real person, disclose.
- YouTube may still apply an automatic label when it detects AI (its own tools,
  C2PA metadata, or internal classifiers). A label is not a penalty and does not
  by itself limit reach or monetization; creators can correct a wrong label in
  Studio → Attributes.

Consistent failure to disclose realistic content can lead to manual labels,
removal, or YPP suspension — not a risk for stick figures, but it is the reason
the "No" answer must be re-checked if the style ever moves toward photorealism.

## 2. Monetization: inauthentic (formerly "repetitious") content

YouTube renamed the policy on 2025-07-15 and clarified that it covers content
that is "repetitive or mass-produced". The current text:

- **Allowed:** "similar content, like a series following a set of characters
  across episodes … but in which each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or
  concept".
- **Not allowed:** "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal
  templates giving the impression of mass production without adding the
  creator's original, authentic insights or perspective", templated storylines,
  and image slideshows with little narrative or educational value.

**For this channel:** the series format itself is fine — the risk is sameness.
A run of episodes that differ only in topic names, with the same beat structure
and identical thumbnails, reads as a template. What keeps it eligible:

- each episode is a distinct researched story with its own arc, not a reskinned
  plot;
- the claims ledger and verification pass are the evidence of educational
  substance — keep them with the published episode;
- vary structure (cold open, framing, ending) so consecutive videos do not feel
  interchangeable;
- do not mass-upload. A burst of near-identical uploads is the pattern
  reviewers look for.

The rule is about substance per video, not about the tool. Using AI to
visualise "a unique character and narrative you invented" is listed as
acceptable.

## 3. Kids and family content

If a video is set as **made for kids**, YouTube applies quality principles on
top of the monetization policies. The ones a story channel can trip:

- _deceptively educational_ — titles or thumbnails promise teaching that the
  video does not deliver;
- _hard to follow_ — jumbled storylines, unclear audio, no beginning/middle/end
  (the page names mass production as a common cause);
- _sensational or misleading_ — exaggerated claims, keyword stuffing.

**For this channel:** the pipeline already targets a grade-4 reading level, but
that is not the same as "made for kids". Decide the audience flag deliberately.
If the flag is kids, the claims ledger matters twice: it is the guard against
"deceptively educational".

## 4. Advertiser-friendly and sensitive topics

- War, death and disaster can limit ads even in stylised form; stick figures
  lower the risk but do not remove it. Keep violence implied, not depicted.
- AI personas presenting themselves as human experts on health, legal, finance
  or politics cannot monetize. Do not let the narrator adopt a "historian
  expert" persona that gives present-day advice; tell the story, do not advise.

## 5. Per-episode checklist

- [ ] AI-use setting reviewed against §1 (expected: No for stick figures).
- [ ] Audience flag chosen deliberately (general vs made for kids).
- [ ] Episode has its own arc; not a plot template with new names.
- [ ] Title/thumbnail claims match what the video teaches.
- [ ] Claims ledger and verification result archived with the episode.
- [ ] No graphic violence; no present-day advice from an AI persona.
- [ ] Upload cadence is sustainable, not a batch dump.
