# Publishing a Story-Forge series on YouTube

Policy notes for a stick-figure animated history channel with synthetic
narration and AI-generated stills. Checked against YouTube's Help Center on
2026-10-20; the platform changes these pages, so re-read the sources before a
launch decision.

## 1. AI disclosure

YouTube requires disclosure only for content that **seems realistic**:

- a real person appears to say or do something they did not do;
- footage of a real event or place is altered;
- a **realistic** scene is generated that did not happen;
- AI-generated **music** is the main focus.

Non-realistic animation is explicitly exempt. The Help Center's own example is
"using an AI-generated or altered animation … in a fully animated video", and
minor assistance — scripts, outlines, thumbnails, captions, upscaling, voice
repair — needs no disclosure either.

**For this channel:** a stick-figure history episode is non-realistic
animation, so the AI-use setting can be "No". Two caveats:

- The synthetic narration is not a cloned real person's voice; if a future
  episode imitates a specific real person, disclose.
- YouTube may still apply an automatic label when it detects AI (its own tools,
  C2PA metadata, or internal classifiers). A label is not a penalty and does not
  by itself limit reach or monetization; creators can correct a wrong label in
  Studio → Attributes.

Consistent failure to disclose realistic content can lead to manual labels,
removal, or YPP suspension — not a risk for stick figures, but it is the reason
the "No" answer must be re-checked if the style ever moves toward photorealism.

## 2. Monetization: inauthentic (formerly "repetitious") content

YouTube renamed the policy on 2025-07-15 and clarified that it covers content
that is "repetitive or mass-produced". The current text:

- **Allowed:** "similar content, like a series following a set of characters
  across episodes … but in which each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or
  concept".
- **Not allowed:** "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal
  templates giving the impression of mass production without adding the
  creator's original, authentic insights or perspective", templated storylines,
  and image slideshows with little narrative or educational value.

**For this channel:** the series format itself is fine — the risk is sameness.
A run of episodes that differ only in topic names, with the same beat structure
and identical thumbnails, reads as a template. What keeps it eligible:

- each episode is a distinct researched story with its own arc, not a reskinned
  plot;
- the claims ledger and verification pass are the evidence of educational
  substance — keep them with the published episode;
- vary structure (cold open, framing, ending) so consecutive videos do not feel
  interchangeable;
- do not mass-upload. A burst of near-identical uploads is the pattern
  reviewers look for.

The rule is about substance per video, not about the tool. Using AI to
visualise "a unique character and narrative you invented" is listed as
acceptable.

## 3. Kids and family content — the "made for kids" decision

YouTube requires every channel to say whether its content is **made for kids**,
as part of an FTC settlement and to comply with COPPA (and local equivalents).
The requirement applies regardless of where the creator lives, and the creator
— not YouTube's classifier — is legally responsible for the answer:

> "As a creator, you know your videos and your audience best, and it is your
> legal responsibility to comply with COPPA and/or other applicable laws, and
> designate your content accurately. Do not rely on our systems to set your
> audience for you."

### 3.1 What makes a video "made for kids"

The Help Center lists the factors; a video is child-directed when children are
the primary audience, **or** when the video is directed at children even if
they are not the only audience ("mixed audience"). The factors that matter for
this channel:

- subject matter — educational content, especially for young children;
- the intended audience as shown in the metadata, or the actual audience;
- child actors or models;
- **characters that appeal to children, "including animated characters or
  cartoon figures"**;
- language intended for children to understand;
- activities that appeal to children — play-acting, songs, games, early
  education;
- **"songs, stories, or poems for children"**;
- advertising aimed at children;
- any other evidence of the audience.

Two clarifications the page makes explicitly:

- "Family friendly" or "safe for everyone" does **not** mean made for kids, and
  children incidentally seeing a video does not make it child-directed. The
  page's own example of general-audience content includes "animated content
  that appeals to everyone".
- Content with sexual themes, violence or other mature themes is likely not
  made for kids.

**For this channel:** a stick-figure history series told as simple stories, in
grade-4 language, with cartoon figures and an educational aim, sits squarely in
the ambiguous zone the page describes — stories, cartoon characters and
child-level language are three of the listed factors. The honest position is
that the audience flag is a **deliberate channel decision**, not something the
pipeline can infer:

- Aim the series at children (or at children plus parents as a mixed audience)
  → set **made for kids**, at channel or video level.
- Aim it at a general/teen+ audience → set **not made for kids**, and keep the
  metadata (titles, thumbnails, descriptions) consistent with that, because
  metadata is one of the factors YouTube weighs. Do not market to children
  while answering "No".

If the answer is genuinely unclear after reading the factors, the page
recommends legal counsel — not a guess.

### 3.2 What the flag changes

For any video set as made for kids, YouTube disables or restricts:

- **comments** (and live chat / Super Chat / donations);
- **personalized advertising** — only non-personalized ads are served, which
  the page notes can reduce revenue;
- **notification bell**;
- **cards and end screens**;
- **save to playlist / watch later**;
- autoplay on home, video watermarks, channel memberships, merchandise and
  ticketing, the miniplayer.

If the whole **channel** is set as made for kids, it additionally loses posts,
channel memberships, the notification bell and "Ask Studio".

**For this channel:** the two that change the product plan are end screens and
comments. A launch plan that assumes a subscribe end screen or a comment-driven
community has to drop that if the flag is "made for kids". Non-personalized ads
also mean the revenue model cannot depend on personalized-ad RPM.

### 3.3 Who sets it, and what happens if it is wrong

- The setting is per channel (applies to existing and future videos) or per
  video, where a video's own setting overrides the channel's.
- YouTube says it may override a setting when it detects error or abuse; the
  video then shows "Set to made for kids" and **cannot be changed** — only
  appealed, once per video.
- Setting it wrongly is a legal exposure under COPPA, not just a platform
  slap: the Help Center repeats that failure may bring FTC action as well as
  YouTube account action.

If the flag is "made for kids", YouTube also applies quality principles on top
of the monetization policies. The ones a story channel can trip:

- _deceptively educational_ — titles or thumbnails promise teaching that the
  video does not deliver;
- _hard to follow_ — jumbled storylines, unclear audio, no beginning/middle/end
  (the page names mass production as a common cause);
- _sensational or misleading_ — exaggerated claims, keyword stuffing.

The pipeline already targets a grade-4 reading level, but that is not the same
as "made for kids". If the flag is kids, the claims ledger matters twice: it is
the guard against "deceptively educational".

## 4. Advertiser-friendly and sensitive topics

- War, death and disaster can limit ads even in stylised form; stick figures
  lower the risk but do not remove it. Keep violence implied, not depicted.
- AI personas presenting themselves as human experts on health, legal, finance
  or politics cannot monetize. Do not let the narrator adopt a "historian
  expert" persona that gives present-day advice; tell the story, do not advise.

## 5. Per-episode checklist

- [ ] AI-use setting reviewed against §1 (expected: No for stick figures).
- [ ] Audience flag chosen deliberately (general vs made for kids) from the
      §3.1 factors — not left to YouTube's classifier.
- [ ] If made for kids: no end screen, no comment-driven plan, non-personalized
      ads only.
- [ ] Episode has its own arc; not a plot template with new names.
- [ ] Title/thumbnail claims match what the video teaches.
- [ ] Claims ledger and verification result archived with the episode.
- [ ] No graphic violence; no present-day advice from an AI persona.
- [ ] Upload cadence is sustainable, not a batch dump.
