THUMBNAIL FORMULA:

VISUAL HOOK:
The image must communicate the video's core curiosity within one second and match the same trigger as the title and narration.

- Shock/scale → make the size contrast obvious.
- Surprise/curiosity → reveal the subject but hide the explanation.
- Debunk → visually contrast the expected belief with reality; use an X or correction cue.
- Fear/stakes → make the threat physically dominate the frame.
- Curiosity gap → show evidence without fully revealing the answer.
- Payoff → visually preview the surprising answer.

Match the supplied Audience Trigger below exactly — a thumbnail that communicates a different trigger than the narration's hook will be rejected as misaligned. Build the visual and the thumbnailText from the fact named in the Trigger Statement, so the thumbnail, the title, and the hook all point at the same fact.

Prefer showing the CAUSE or MECHANISM when the narration explains a cause → mechanism → result story, rather than merely showing the final result.

THUMBNAIL TEXT RULES:
- thumbnailText is 2–5 words (at most 30 characters total, counting spaces), ALL CAPS, no exclamation marks, no emojis, no hashtags.
- It must come from the Trigger Statement or the Hook. Use a number, a contrast, or the open question — never a claim that goes beyond the narration.
- It must reinforce the trigger the Title carries. It must NOT simply repeat the Title word for word, and it must not pull in a different direction from the Title (for example "WHY?" under a scale title).
- If the trigger fact is debated or unverified, the text must not state it as settled (use a question or "THEORY"-style framing rather than a flat claim).
- Avoid generic text such as "SHOCKING", "WATCH THIS", "YOU WON'T BELIEVE".

TYPOGRAPHY:
Typography is a major visual element.

Use huge ALL-CAPS condensed heavy fonts similar to Anton, Bebas Neue ExtraBold, Impact, or Oswald Heavy.

Preferred treatment:
- 2–5 words maximum.
- 2–4 stacked lines when appropriate.
- Extremely large and mobile-readable.
- White for supporting words.
- Yellow for the strongest curiosity/emphasis word.
- Red for danger, contradiction, or consequence.
- Thick black outline/shadow.
- Distressed texture and rough red/yellow paint-brush backing when useful.
- Strong hierarchy and tight spacing.
- Typography should feel like a viral documentary/news thumbnail, not subtitles or a poster title.
- Never use thin, elegant, or small typography.
