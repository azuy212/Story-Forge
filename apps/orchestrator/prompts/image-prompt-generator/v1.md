## Scene 1 visual trigger delivery (mandatory)

Scene 1 is the first frame the viewer sees. It must visually deliver the audience-retention trigger the script's hook set up. The PromptQA check verifies this on every generation; you will be re-issued if the visual does not match the trigger.

Match the scene 1 prompt to the trigger the script's first sentence lands. Read the scene's `visualDescription` and `narration` for the trigger hint, then choose the matching composition:

- **Shock / scale:** dramatic close-up OR extreme wide that shows the scale, subject dwarfed by the phenomenon, expected-vs-actual contrast. Not a neutral establishing shot.
- **Surprise / curiosity:** subject partially obscured, off-frame element implied, dramatic negative space, unusual perspective. The image must NOT answer the question the hook asks.
- **Past-belief-challenge / debunk:** the wrong belief visibly crossed out, before/after split, expected vs actual side-by-side, or a clear visual contrast between belief and reality. Not a neutral illustration of the topic.
- **Fear / stakes:** high-contrast danger lighting, low-angle or close-up framing, subject dwarfed by the threat. Not a documentary stock shot.
- **Curiosity gap (open question with pay-off later):** subject hidden, off-frame, or context-stripped. The image poses the question, does not answer it.
- **Documentary-explanation arc with no specific trigger:** still use a strong visual hook — dominant subject, high contrast, dramatic composition. Avoid a generic, low-detail establishing shot.

When in doubt, the visual must MATCH the emotional weight of the first narration sentence. A viewer who mutes the audio should still see the trigger in scene 1 within one second.

Scene type adaptation guidelines (use ONLY these sceneType values):

- map: Detailed cartographic style, clean borders, political/geographic overlays, documentary cartography aesthetic
- landscape: Vast geographic setting, atmospheric photorealistic scenery, golden-hour or dramatic documentary lighting
- macro: Extreme close-up, shallow depth of field, fine texture and detail, scientific or natural-world macro aesthetic
- wildlife: Documentary wildlife photography, natural habitat framing, photorealistic animal/plant subjects
- historical: Period reconstruction, archival atmosphere, era-appropriate lighting and materiality, documentary realism
- diagram: Educational vector illustration, labeled elements, clean white or dark background, textbook quality
- comparison: Side-by-side or split-screen contrast, consistent lighting across both halves, clear visual juxtaposition
- portrait: Historical or documentary portrait, period-appropriate lighting, textured background, cinematic shallow depth of field
- timelapse: Long-exposure/motion-sweep aesthetic, celestial or weather-scale movement, cinematic documentary quality
- infographic: Minimal documentary infographic, clean data visualization, scientific journal aesthetic
- reconstruction: CGI/film-quality reconstruction of an event or structure, historically informed, photorealistic render
- animation: Animated explainer aesthetic, clean shapes, consistent lighting, motion-graphics documentary style

## SceneRole & VisualAnchor contract (code-derived, mandatory)

sceneType, sceneRole, and visualAnchor are assigned by the pipeline from the narration's content and retention architecture. Treat them as structural constraints, not suggestions:

- sceneType: derived from the narration segment's content; honor it in the composition (see guidelines below).
- sceneRole:
  - narrative: content-bearing scenes that advance the story — full cinematic treatment per the scene's visualDescription and the visual plan.
  - b-roll: short transitional/atmospheric cutaways — keep the prompt simple and secondary: no new narrative information, supporting mood or context only. Do not turn a b-roll cutaway into a key moment.
- visualAnchor: when adjacent scenes share a visualAnchor, the subject, diagram, map, or object must stay visually continuous (same identity, layout, and framing) across those scenes. Do not swap the anchor for a new unrelated image mid-explanation.

Output structure:

- assets: array of asset prompt objects, one per scene
  - sceneId: must match the provided scene ID
  - assetType: "image" for static visuals
  - generationPrompt: rich cinematic prompt (40+ characters)
