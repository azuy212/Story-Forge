## Scene 1 visual trigger delivery (mandatory)

Scene 1 is the first frame the viewer sees. It must visually deliver the audience-retention trigger the script's hook set up. The PromptQA check verifies this on every generation; you will be re-issued if the visual does not match the trigger.

Match the scene 1 prompt to the trigger the script's first sentence lands. Read the scene's `visualDescription` and `narration` for the trigger hint, then choose the matching composition:

- **Shock / scale:** dramatic close-up OR extreme wide that shows the scale, subject dwarfed by the phenomenon, expected-vs-actual contrast. Not a neutral establishing shot.
- **Surprise / curiosity:** subject partially obscured, off-frame element implied, dramatic negative space, unusual perspective. The image must NOT answer the question the hook asks.
- **Past-belief-challenge / debunk:** the wrong belief visibly crossed out, before/after split, expected vs actual side-by-side, or a clear visual contrast between belief and reality. Not a neutral illustration of the topic.
- **Fear / stakes:** high-contrast danger lighting, low-angle or close-up framing, subject dwarfed by the threat. Not a documentary stock shot.
- **Curiosity gap (open question with pay-off later):** subject hidden, off-frame, or context-stripped. The image poses the question, does not answer it.
- **Documentary-explanation arc with no specific trigger:** still use a strong visual hook — dominant subject, high contrast, dramatic composition. Avoid a generic, low-detail establishing shot.

When in doubt, the visual must MATCH the emotional weight of the first narration sentence. A viewer who mutes the audio should still see the trigger in scene 1 within one second.

Scene type adaptation guidelines:

- map: Detailed cartographic style, clean borders, political/geographic overlays, documentary cartography aesthetic

- logo: Clean logo reveal background, minimalist, brand-appropriate colors, subtle lighting
