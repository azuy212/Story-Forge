Scene rules:

1. Create a number of scenes inside the Scene Count Range {{sceneCountRange}} (aim for {{targetSceneCount}}). Each scene is a single visual idea.
2. Do not create one scene per sentence — narration sentences do NOT map 1:1 to scenes. Combine related sentences into coherent visual ideas. Merge adjacent narrative beats when necessary for visual coherence, but never let a story beat disappear (see sourceBeatIds).
3. Scene pacing must follow natural narration pacing, not equal segmentation.
4. Pacing guidelines (the system derives exact timestamps from your segment lengths):
   - Hook (opening): {{speakingRateWordsPerSecond}} words/second pacing
   - Reveal: proportional to narration word count
   - Explanations: proportional to narration word count
   - Ending: proportional to narration word count
5. Every scene should reference at least one approved fact ID from the Approved Facts list. B-roll/transitional scenes may reference none.
6. Camera variety: avoid consecutive scenes with identical cameraShot AND cameraMotion. Avoid consecutive scenes with identical sceneType unless they share a visualAnchor (a persistent diagram or map may repeat its sceneType while the camera changes).
7. Look variety versus continuity: scenes that share a visualAnchor keep the SAME renderStyle and the SAME colorMood (so the subject stays visually continuous), and may vary lighting, composition, and camera. Change colorMood and lighting between scenes only when the visualAnchor changes or at a story-beat boundary.
8. Reference only fact IDs listed in Approved Facts. Do not invent fact IDs.
9. Emotional pacing: plan an emotional arc across the video, not just a visual one. Each scene gets one emotionalBeat, and the sequence should build intentionally. Use this ladder, in order: 1 mystery → 2 tension → 3 discovery → 4 awe → 5 payoff → 6 relief or reflection. Adjacent scenes may hold the same step or move forward by ONE step (skipping a step is not allowed). A step backward is allowed only at a story-beat boundary and must be motivated in `sceneGoal` (what the new beat resets). Open at mystery, tension, or awe, not at payoff. Avoid monotone sequences (all scenes the same emotionalBeat).
10. Match emotionalBeat to the content's classification where relevant: a debated or popular-theory fact fits "mystery"/"tension"; a verified reveal fits "discovery"/"payoff"; a myth-debunk fits "revelation"-style "payoff".
11. If Narrative Ending is provided, make final scene narration end with its exact narration, use emotionalBeat "payoff" or "reflection", and follow its visualDirection. Do not create a scene after the ending.
12. Scene 1 (the hook) must visually deliver the Audience Trigger supplied below. Choose a sceneType, cameraShot, visualDescription, and visualAnchor that make the trigger legible in the first second with audio muted:
     - shock: show the scale/contrast directly (subject dwarfed, expected-vs-actual).
     - surprise / curiosity: conceal the explanation — partial occlusion, dramatic negative space, off-frame subject.
     - past-belief-challenge: depict the wrong belief being corrected (before/after, crossed-out, side-by-side contrast).
     - fear / stakes: high-contrast danger framing, threat dominating the frame.
     - greed: a close visual preview of the "one thing" the video reveals.
     - insecurity: visually contrast the common misconception with the reality.
     - documentary-explanation: still use a strong, high-contrast, dominant-subject hook.
13. Narration splitting:
     - The provided narration is already finalized.
     - Your job is only to divide it into scene segments.
     - Never generate new narration.
     - Never remove words.
     - Never add explanations.
     - Never change sentence structure.
     - Copy every segment exactly, including punctuation. Join your scene narrations in order and check that they reproduce the original narration exactly once.
     - If a segment is reworded, the system will re-segment the original narration automatically, so keep your segment boundaries as close to the intended scene content as possible.
14. Visual anchors carry continuity (see the anchor rules below): every `sceneRole: narrative` scene must declare a `visualAnchor`; omit it only for b-roll/atmospheric scenes.
15. Pivot emphasis: the scene containing the supplied Pivot Sentence is the moment muted-feed scrollers leave. Give that scene `emphasis: "high"` and the strongest available visual contrast — a scale shift, an expected-vs-actual split, or a camera-motion reversal (a push-in where the previous scene pulls back, a static where it moved). It must NOT reuse the following scene's `visualAnchor` unchanged: the pivot is the sanctioned moment to break the anchor.
16. Bookend shot: the final scene must reuse the Scene-1 `visualAnchor` string exactly — the audience reads "we are back at the start, but now it means something different", which is the strongest linearity signal available in short form. The only exception is Ending Type `twist`, where the anchor must change because the opening's meaning has been overturned. Because of this, choose a Scene-1 anchor that is simple, concrete, and easy to return to (one subject or one object, not a complicated diagram).

Anchor rules (keep these consistent with the transition cap):

- Adjacent narrative scenes that explain the same mechanism, system, comparison, or causal chain MUST use the identical visualAnchor string.
- Plan few anchors: usually 3 or 4 across the whole video, changing at story-beat boundaries. Do not invent a new anchor for every scene.
- Every anchor change between adjacent narrative scenes needs a declared reason. Any ONE of these is enough:
  1. a transition other than `cut` (counts toward the transition cap below);
  2. the scenes belong to different story beats (different sourceBeatIds) and the new scene's `sceneGoal` begins with "New beat:" and says which anchor is replaced;
  3. the new scene is the pivot scene, and its `sceneGoal` begins with "Pivot:";
  4. the new scene is the final scene returning to the Scene-1 anchor, and its `sceneGoal` begins with "Bookend:".
- A narrative scene with no anchor, or an anchor change with no declared reason, is a defect.

Transition semantics — the enum is not a free choice:

- cut — the default inside one anchor/idea. Use it freely between sentences of the same visual.
- match-cut — allowed ONLY when consecutive scenes share a shape, position, or motion correspondence, and you state that correspondence in the `visualDescription` of BOTH scenes.
- cross-dissolve / fade — reserved for time jumps, location changes, or a beat-boundary mood shift. Never mid-explanation.
- zoom — emphasis reveals only.
- Hard cap: at most 2 non-cut transitions in any 60 seconds of runtime. More than that reads as a music video and contradicts the documentary tone. Spend them on the biggest shifts (usually the pivot and the bookend return); use the declared-reason routes above for other anchor changes.
