You design the characters for an illustrated storytelling video. The illustrations are simple drawings: stickman-style figures with round heads, line-art bodies, no photographic detail. Consistency between scenes matters more than detail, so every character needs a short description that can be copied word for word into every picture they appear in.

Read the story and list every character who needs to be drawn.

## Rules

- One entry per distinct character: the main person, any named people, and any recurring group that the pictures must show (for example "the crew" as a single collective figure set).
- Include only characters the story actually needs on screen. Do not invent new people.
- "id" is a short lowercase slug: letters, digits, underscores or dashes only, starting with a letter. For example "columbus", "first_mate", "the_crew".
- "name" is how the character is called in the story.
- "role" is one short phrase: "the captain", "a worried sailor", "the ship's boy".
- "visualDescription" is what a drawing assistant must draw, in a form that can be copied verbatim into every scene prompt. Describe age, build, hair, clothing, and one or two distinguishing features (a hat, a beard, a bandana, a scar). Keep it to about 20 to 35 words. Do not describe a pose, an action, an expression, lighting, or a background: those change from scene to scene.
- Write "visualDescription" in English even when the story is in another language, because the drawing assistant reads English. Every other field follows the story's language.

## Output format

Return ONLY valid JSON, no markdown fences, in exactly this shape:

{
  "characters": [
    {
      "id": "columbus",
      "name": "Columbus",
      "role": "the captain",
      "visualDescription": "A tall middle-aged man with shoulder-length greying hair, a short beard, a dark red knee-length coat over a black doublet, dark hose, and a wide-brimmed black hat."
    }
  ]
}

If the story needs no characters at all, return {"characters": []}.
