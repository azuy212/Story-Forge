# Editorial Guidelines

Apply these rules to all content for "Universe Decoded."

## Hook First

- Every video must begin with a curiosity/shock hook — a genuinely interesting fact from the story, delivered before any context. This is not clickbait; it stays factual and documentary in tone.
- Never begin a video with:
  - a date ("Boston, January 15, 1919...")
  - a location ("In the city of Boston...")
  - "In [year]..."
  - "Scientists discovered..."
  - "Today we're going to..."
  - generic historical context
  - "Have you ever wondered...", "What if I told you...", or any "you won't believe" phrasing
- Date and location belong after the hook, as context.
- Follow the structure: Hook → context → escalation → explanation → consequence → memorable ending.
- The ending must visibly complete the hook's promise (question → answer, belief → correction, threat → outcome) or honestly leave a labeled open question.

## Tone

- Documentary tone — informative, measured, engaging
- No hype, no sensationalism, no exclamation marks
- No emojis
- No excessive capitalization (thumbnail text is the one exception)

## Content Quality

- Every claim based on real information
- Do not fabricate facts, statistics, or quotes
- One core idea per video
- Optimised for the target duration of the format (45-60 seconds for a Short)
- Certainty must match the evidence: verified facts are stated plainly; likely, debated, theory, and unverified claims always carry attribution or uncertainty language

## Vocabulary

- Simple, clear language, accessible to a global audience (about CEFR B1 to lower B2)
- Explain jargon briefly where unavoidable
- Active voice preferred
- Short sentences, mostly 8–15 words, never above about 25
- Write for the ear: numbers and units a voice can say naturally, no symbols or abbreviations a voice engine may misread

## Audience Triggers

Every script should use the strongest honest audience-retention angle supported by the research. A distinct trigger is preferred, not mandatory when the facts do not support one; in that case, use a clear documentary-explanation arc (see the safety rules below). The acceptable triggers are the **honest** versions of the seven below; the unsafe interpretations are explicitly forbidden. Triggers must come from research facts — never invented.

| Trigger                   | Honest version (use this)                                                               | Forbidden version (never use)                                            |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Curiosity**             | An open question, an unresolved implication, or a pay-off delayed across beats.         | Manufactured mystery, "nobody knows why" as a dead end.                  |
| **Surprise**              | A counter-intuitive fact stated before context.                                         | A trivial fact dressed up as shocking.                                   |
| **Shock**                 | A scale, contrast, or contradiction with real weight.                                   | Cheap shock — gore, fear-mongering, or any unverifiable claim.           |
| **Past-belief-challenge** | A verified `myth` or `false` fact debunked with evidence.                               | A widely-held belief attacked without evidence.                          |
| **Fear**                  | A real, research-supported risk or consequence framed with stakes.                      | Manufactured panic, personal-safety fear without verified grounding.     |
| **Greed**                 | A genuine value-density or pay-off preview (what the viewer gains by watching).         | Empty promise of reward, "you'll be rich / healed / transformed".        |
| **Insecurity**            | **Gap awareness** — pointing out a common misconception, mistake, or missing knowledge. | Personal anxiety provocation (status, appearance, finances, body image). |

**Safety rules (non-negotiable):**

- Every trigger must be derived from a research fact, not invented.
- Triggers must not weaponize protected characteristics, vulnerable groups, or unverifiable claims.
- Clickbait and overselling are still clickbait even when wrapped in trigger language. The "no hype, no sensationalism" rule above still applies.
- When the research does not support a specific trigger, a documentary-explanation arc with clear questions, causal links, and a satisfying payoff is the correct choice — do not force a trigger the research cannot anchor.

**One trigger, one fact, every surface.** The title, hook, thumbnail text, description opener, pinned comment, and opening visual must all point at the same trigger fact. Different wording is fine; different facts or opposite directions are not.

## Visual Rules

- Generated images contain no readable text, labels, logos, or watermarks. Titles are added by the thumbnail step or by a compositor.
- Do not depict real, named, or recognizable people unless a source image is supplied for them; use anonymous or non-identifiable figures instead.
- No gore or graphic injury. Show scale, aftermath, and stakes instead.

# Enums

All enum fields must use exactly one of the provided options.
Never invent new values or variations.

Fact classification values: verified, likely, debated, popular_theory, unverified_claim, myth, false. (There is no "unresolved_popular_theory" value; use popular_theory.)
